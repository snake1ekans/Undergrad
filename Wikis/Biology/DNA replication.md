The process of creating new [[DNA]], especially in [[Mitosis]], using [[DNA polymerase]]. This is done with the goal of making complete copies of the [[Genome]] in preparation for cell division so that each [[Daughter Cell||daughter cell]] has the same traits as the parent. Thus, an essential aspect of DNA replication is complete fidelity to the given genetic material. In cells with [[Telomere|telomeres]] (and linear genomes), making sure that telomeres don't shrink is also essential.

# Process
An [[Initiator]] (DNAA) sequence recognizes ORI sequences and binds to them, unwinding the DNA and recruiting proteins to the replication site. [[Helicase]] then breaks the hydrogen bonds to open the DNA template. Just below the helicase, single-stranded DNA binding proteins bind to the strands, keeping them from re-annealing and allowing them to be replicated. [[Primase]] then synthezises small RNA sequences. [[DNA polymerase]] then adds [[Nucleotide|nucleotides]] to the strand according to the template.

# Essential aspects
There are several descriptive essential aspects of DNA replication.
## Semi-Conservative Replication
The model of DNA replication, in which one original and one new strand are integrated into a replicated DNA strand. Proven by the Meselson-Stahl experiment.
### Meselson-Stahl
MS [[Pulse Chase Experiment]] showed that replication is semi-conservative by growing bacterial cells with heavy nitrogen, then switched cells to regular nitrogen, then centrifuged the cells to see if they have heavy or light DNA.
### Disproven models
#### Conservative replication
A model in which the old copy of DNA remains intact and a new identical strand is made in its place
#### Dispersive replication
A model in which fragments of the new and old copy are mixed together in a motley chromosome.
## Bidirectional
DNA replication is bidirectional, meaning that in circular genomes, a small break is formed between the 2 strands and then the DNA is replicated in both directions at the same time before separating. %%>Replication happens both directions%%
## Semi-discontinuous 
DNA replication, in addition to being semi-conservative, is semi-discontinuous. Because [[Nucleic Acid|nucleic acids]] can only be synthesized 5' to 3', one half of the [[DNA]] has to be replicated differently. DNA runs antiparallel, so as one side is being replicated 5' to 3' (the "leading" strand), the other strand is being run through the process 3' to 5' (the "lagging" strand). This was shown through Okazaki's pulse-chase experiment. %%>Leading is replicated normally, lagging makes okazaki fragments%%
### Okazaki Fragments
Since the lagging strand cannot be replicated normally, small fragments, called okazaki fragments, are formed on the daughter strand.

# Elements and Enzymes
A list of all the physical components of DNA replication, ordered upstream -> downstream following the leading strand, followed by the lagging strand.
## Topoisomerase
Nicks the Phosphate backbone of the DNA upstream of [[#DNAA]] in order to relieve the pressure created by unwinding the DNA. 
## Origins of Replication
Origins of replication (ORIs) are [[Arginine|A]]:[[Thymine|T]] rich regions of DNA where replication begins. 
## DNAA
The initiator sequence that unwinds the DNA strand
## Helicase
It unzips the [[Gene|genes]] (hyuk hyuk). Breaks the [[Hydrogen Bond|hydrogen bonds]] between the base pairs, which separates the 2 strands.
## ssDNA
The single-stranded DNA binders that bind just below the helicase split, which allows the DNA to replicate without being [[Annealing|reannealed]].
## Primase
Creates small strands of [[RNA]] that [[Primer|prime]] the site for replication.
## DNA Polymerase
There are 2 main types of [[DNA polymerase]]: polymerase 3 and polymerase 1
### Polymerase III
Polymerase 3 exists as a dimer and does most of the synthesis. The leading strand polymerase does essentially all the synthesis on its strand, and the lagging polymerase synthesizes the [[#Okazaki Fragments]] on the lagging strand. Polymerase 3 has a proofreading capacity to catch mismatches and fix them. %%>Most of the work; can proofread%%
### Polymerase I
The synthesis polymerase that is responsible for connecting the [[#Okazaki Fragments]] formed by removing the primers (thanks RNAse H!). It has the same proofreading capacity as polymerase 3. After reading the last nucleotide left by RNAse H, it removes it and fills in the gap. %%>Fill in Okazaki gaps%%
## RNAse H
Finds and removes all of the RNA primers created by [[#Primase]]. It frees all except the last [[Nucleotide]], which is read by polymerase I.
## DNA Ligase
[[DNA Ligase]] then makes [[Phosphodiester Bonds]] to seal DNA fragments together, which seals the deal and erases any trace of Okazaki fragments
## Telomerase
As linear DNA is replicated, the strands get progressively shorter, due to primase's inability to lay down an RNA primer at the very end of a strand. To combat this issue, [[Telomere|telomeres]] are created using [[Telomerase]]
