The chemicals that encode information in [[Nucleic Acid]]. Bases are hydrophobic, which means they tend to associate with themselves. The 5 are: [[Adenine]], [[Guanine]], [[Cytosine]], [[Thymine]] ([[DNA]]), and [[Uracil]] ([[RNA]]). Their respective codes are: A, G, C, T, and U. Y is for Pyrimidines, R for Purines, and N for any base. 
___
# Categories
## Pyrimidines
The single-ringed bases: [[Thymine]], [[Uracil]], and [[Cytosine]]. Chemically, all pyrimidines have a hydrogenated 1 and oxygenated 2. In addition, 
- Cytosine has an amide (NH$_2$) group
- Thymine has a hydrogenated 3, oxygenated 4, and [[Methylation|methylated]] 5. 
- Uracil has a hydrogenated 3 and oxygenated 4. 
## Purines
The double-ringed bases: [[Adenine]] and [[Guanine]]. Both have a hydrogenated 9, but: 
- Adenine has an amidated 6.
- Guanine has an amidated 2 and oxygenated 6. 
## Differentiation
Given a diagram, It's quite easy to remember which bases are which with 3 simple rules:
C-G bond at 3 sites, A-T only bond at 2, and only hydrogens not attached to the carbon ring will participate in hydrogen bonding.
___
# Bonding

Bases always bond to [[NA sugar|sugars]] on the sugar's 1 and the base's 1 or 9 nitrogen 

## Bond numbers
Cytosine and Guanine form triple bonds, as their amine and oxygen groups are more [[Electronegativity|electronegtive]] than Adenine and Thymine/Uracil.
