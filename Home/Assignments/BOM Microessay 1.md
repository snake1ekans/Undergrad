Topic: #4 - Power phrases
Passage: title page of BoM

Essay:

This week being the introductory week, I decided to focus on the title page of the Book of Mormon. While I admittedly have studied it a few times without really understanding its broader context within the sacred text (as it was translated as opposed to later added by the church), I chose to focus on the power phrases. 

This was a rather apropos choice given Moroni's particularly punchy style in the introduction. Knowing that it was written (engraved?) as an introduction to his abridgment of plates allowed me to more fully explore the phrases contained within. Every time that I read the title page, the first phrase that jumps out at me is "Written by way of commandment." While this may just seem like a pretty bog-standard phrase to describe "scripture," it takes on a deeper meaning when applied to a book that wasn't compiled from several different writings and declared holy (a la the Bible or Pearl of Great Price), but rather was specifically commanded. 

I also love the implications that this has for each individual author. We're shown in the end of the plates of Nephi that the authors became rather lazy when it came to writing their spiritual history (presumably keeping their secular history better-recorded on the large plates). In a meta way, the distinction between the dense theological treatises of Nephi and the measly book of Omni, which covers one page and more history than the rest of the small plates combined, demonstrates the exact spiritual laziness that allowed the Nephites to apostatize and mingle with the Mulekites.

This significance derives from a knowledge of the text at large, but what doesn't is the next phrase that jumped out at me on this particular reading: "The interpretation thereof by the Gift of God." While this sentence is likely about the "interpretation" of the reformed egyptian done by Joseph Smith, I find this sentence has a second spiritual sense: that the text itself is interpreted by us using spiritual gifts, which ultimately flow from God. 

Often in my mission I would have people ask me academic questions regarding this element or that, but often the answers that they received were not what they sought. The truth is that academic interpretation is usually in tension with the spiritual interpretation. For example, this passage. 

The academic interpretation would say that it refers to the translation process, evidenced by the preceding passage talking about the Book of Mormon's coming forth. On the other hand, the spiritual interpretation is one based on my experiences with the Book of Mormon. That those moments in which I understand it the most are times in which I am filled with the spirit, the gift that I received as a part of my baptismal covenant. I was able to share that again with my mother this Sunday as we talked about how our understanding of scripture and the world shapes our understanding of doctrines like the Priesthood.

These two ideas aren't mutually exclusive, the spiritual  doesn't negate the academic or vice versa, but rather they exist as 2 methods by which we can glean meaning from the scripture: with the context given by my knowledge of ancient Judaism and the language of the Book of Mormon, and with my lived spiritual experiences testifying to me that it is true. 

There's more that I could say, but even in the first paragraph of the first page of the Book of Mormon there is such a trove of spiritual meaning that I am inevitably brought by the spirit and my brain to conclude that it is scripture.
