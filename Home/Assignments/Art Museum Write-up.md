# 1 - Jesus and Mary: The Moment After
This painting is a really good reminder of the tangibility of the resurrection. Most depictions of the resurrection have Christ as almost an ethereal, surreal figure, but this painting instead makes Christ and Mary the most tangible things in the art. Instead, the focus on the surreal is instead presented in the background and in the flowers, which represent the purity of Christ and the mortality of Mary.

As strange as it sounds, I really liked the art because of the ways that Christ and Mary are painted: It reminds me of a recent Mormon Horror film about the start of polygamy, but in this case I think the thing that I like the most is that it reminds me of the ways in which Christ's time and place were foreign and even fear-inducing to us, but the flowers remind us that the universality of Christ's message and resurrection transcend both physical time and space (as shown by their seeming separation from the world of the painting.)

I think the thing I learned most from this painting is the way in which Christ's atonement was tangible to those around him, and yet its power and efficacy spans time and space, providing an infinite source of power for atonement.

# 2 - Lift Up Thine Eyes
This painting is a slightly different one from the previous, and probably has the most famous name attached to it: Norman Rockwell. This is a painting more about the ways in which we may ignore Christ's influence in our daily lives. I think the most important symbolism of this is the downturned eyes of all the passersby, combined with the flying flock of pigeons (doves) which symbolize the omnipresence of Christ in our lives.

While I'm not a huge art aficionado, I appreciate the mixing of contemporary cartoonish styles of the populace and doves with the hyper-real art of the church building itself. It's a reminder of the more glorious and detailed things that come of our lives when we "lift up our eyes" and direct them toward more spiritual things in our on the daily. 

I think the biggest thing I learned/was reminded of from this painting was the ease with which we can slip back into our routines and lose sight of the things that are important. While the people weren't going to fly away with the doves, they were looking down and losing themselves in wherever their rush brought them next. It's a lesson that I think I can learn from, even here at the Lord's University

# 3 - Rescue
Evoking a visceral reaction from me is rare, but I am not one to deny the power of good art, and this certainly evoked a reaction from me. The painting isn't of Christ specifically, but rather a saving angel. It's a very powerful message, especially given the child in the angel's arms. It teaches us about the universality of Christ's message. 

This painting evoked quite a reaction from me. As I was pondering this painting, I was reminded of the pain wrought on so many people throughout the world, especially in the holy land, by men who seek power. The angel descending to carry the child can be seen as a rescue to return them to this temporal world or a rescue from the man-made hell that our warmongering greed and hubris inflicted. It causes me to ponder on the angels that descended on the girls' school in Iran, bombed earlier this year by the United States. 

This painting is a reminder of Christ's mercy. That despite the evil that is wrought on the world, there is peace and rest for the innocents of the world, and those that choose to follow Christ, as we pursue the edification of zion on earth and a community of participants in the divine nature both on this mortal plane and on the other side of the veil. May God bless our souls, and may he save us all.
