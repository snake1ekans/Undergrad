The Restored Church of Jesus Christ, like all religion and spirituality, operates on principles of faith. The existence of God is an empirically subjective one, and thus the existence of God is not objectively provable. While this may seem an issue for some, the Lord reassures us through his prophet Alma by proclaiming: 

"Now I ask, is this faith? Behold, I say unto you, Nay; for if a man knoweth a thing he hath no cause to believe, for he knoweth it." (Alma 34)

Thus, Alma explains how this lack of provability is important. Our knowledge of God is personal and tender, not cold and calculated, and our relationship with him equally important. As LDS scholar Blake Ostler has said:

"If the oneness of the Son and/or Holy Ghost with the Father should cease, then so would their divinity... it is inconceivable that the Father should be God in isolation from them because God is literally the love of the divine persons for each other."

While the nature of God is imprecisely known and defined, we can learn much of him from his inspired words to his chosen messengers, Prophets. While some would argue that those words are perfectly transmitted, there are a few intermediary steps that would lead us to believe that Prophets are not clear glass through which God's light may shine, but rather dirty mirrors (as the Apostle Paul explained) through which the light of God may shine, but not in its purest and most truthful form.

Thus, the question of Prophetic Fallibility is posed. How can a perfect God allow his word to be passed through imperfect people? By what mechanisms is the mirror made dirty? How can a church that is guided by living prophets say that those prophets can err so greatly?

In this paper, I will seek to explain how one can understand more fully the nature of revelation in the church by exploring a common traditional explanation, examining the implications of the explanation and explaining the harm of those implications. I will examine scriptural, doctrinal, and scholarly explanations and evidences and propose a framework through which we can better seek to understand the nature of divine communication. 

# A Common Latter-Day Saint Apologetic

A frequently misquoted saying of Joseph Smith, "a prophet was a prophet only when he was acting as such.” is often cited by Latter-Day saints as a sort of litmus test or explanation for the foibles of man. While this quote is of dubious origin, it is also taken out of context. Citing History of the Church (which suffers from a litany of historiographical issues), Bruce R McConkie states:

// 'They have their opinions and prejudices and are left to work out their problems without inspiration in many instances. Joseph Smith recorded that he ‘visited with a brother and sister from Michigan, who thought that “a prophet is always a prophet”; but I told them that a prophet was a prophet only when he was acting as such.'"'

While the quote in context casts aspersion on the very notion of a "revelation or humanity" model, frequently Latter-Day Saints cling to "only when acting as such" and cast the rest aside. While this is an issue with Christianity as a whole and conjoins nicely with the Protestant Doctrine of sola scriptura, discussions about the historical nature of how Sola Scriptura influenced this notion in Latter-Day Saints is best left to historians.

The apologetic at work: Brigham Young implements a dubious policy of restricting the priesthood from men of African ancestry? "only when acting as such." Brigham Young teaches a poorly-elaborated theory about the shedding of one's own blood for full atonement? "only when acting as such." Brigham Young teaches that Adam is in some way God the Father? "only when acting as such."

(While other wildly out of context statements from other church leaders are readily available, Brigham Young was particularly impactful as a notoriously fiery and off-the cuff orator. This, combined with unreliable record-keeping of his speeches and a public feud, or beef if you will, with the Apostle Orson Pratt led to some interesting teachings.)

While this reconciliation is a technically valid option, it can often lead to more questions than it answers. They are discussed below:

# The Troubles 
## The Problem of Discernment
A succinct distillation of the problem of Discernment is this: If God speaks perfectly through his Prophets often but not always, then how can one know if the words come from the Human or the Divine? Essentially, under the framework of "Perfect or Human?" We are left to guess. 

While discernment is a spiritual gift for our day and is important in several contexts, it becomes absolutely necessary in this context. If God has commanded you to do something, then you must do it, otherwise you are disobeying the Word of God. However, our answers often come later than hoped or often never at all. This flows well into our next implication.

## The Problem of Dismissal
Great, you think you've discerned that x statement is "human" or "false" or a dreaded "philosophy of man" (the most terrifying of philosophies). What now? Do you ignore it and go on with your life? Do you actively speak out against it? Do you dare criticize the Church because you and you alone have truly discerned that the policy in place is actually false doctrine? (the most terrifying of doctrines)

A perfect case study: the priesthood ban aforementioned. It has presented a stumbling block for many a member/catechumen/scholar/missionary of the Church. While I can argue that I personally don't believe it's inspired based on the available evidence, I don't find it damaging for the truth claims of the church. Others believe that it was, in fact, inspired. Operating of the framework of "perfect or not," one of us is wrong, and either option creates implications when defending the church. Either God was temporarily racist, or he allowed such a policy to be in place for several years under this framework.
## The Problem of Perfection
The problem of Dismissal runs parallel to the problem of Perfection: If God is speaking perfectly to prophets, why all the little changes? Why so many endowment changes? Garment changes? Church service changes? Why doesn't God just perfectly reveal exactly how we need to do it the first time? 

An excellent example of this is the presentation of the endowment, a sacred ceremony that has undergone numerous changes throughout the history of the Restored Church. Several of these changes remove elements of Masonic ceremony from the endowment.
Courtesy of Mormonr:

"In a letter to Parley P. Pratt in June 1842, Heber C. Kimball said that "there is a similarity of priesthood in Masonry." In 1858 he said that:

> We have the true Masonary. The Masonary of the day is received from the apostasy which took place in the days of Solomon and David. They have now and then a thing that is correct, but we have the real thing, but how many comprehend it and see the propriety of being true towards each other?
"

Despite this sentiment that the Endowment represented a "True Masonry," the changes made have steadily removed elements of masonry from the ceremony itself. Under a black-and-white model, the Endowment couldn't have actually been revelation due to the fragmentary nature of its changes and the steady removal of "True" elements.
# What do the scriptures actually tell us?

The following passages are useful in principle to explain a more LDS understanding of scripture and revelation. After all, scripture is the crystallization of revelation past.  These are not proof-texts in any regard, but rather scriptures that teach principles about scripture, prophets, and prophecy. 

## What is the nature of Scripture?
The concept of scripture is a surprisingly messy one. While "the scriptures" is broadly defined as "what's in the LDS Quad," most Latter-Day Saints would not describe certain passages as "scripture," such as the song of songs and the JSH. Thus, a definition of "Scripture" as a concept is needed. The most instructive principles about the nature of "scripture" can be found in 2 passages: 2 Timothy 3:16, and D&C 68:4. 

2 Timothy reads "Every scripture is 'θεόπνευστος' and is useful to teaching, reproof, correction, and for instruction in righteousness" (Wayment's NT). I have chosen to "untranslate" one of the words in the passage due to its very tricky nature. There aren't any other instances of Theopneustos (translated as "inspired by God" in Wayment's NT) in the bible, and thus many different interpretations exist. A particular interpretation that I favor is "life-giving," or the idea that passages that breathe Godly life into us are "scripture."

Another crucial passage is found in the Doctrine and Covenants, in which it is recorded "whatsoever (thing) [an elder] shall speak when moved upon by the holy [spirit] shall be scripture..." While this passage at first blush could also support the above model, the key idea of "moved upon" or "inspired" would indicate not a perfect transmission of word-for-word thoughts of God, but rather thoughts of man with a holy "stamp of approval."

The scriptures teach not that they are the "direct locutions of the Divine," to quote scholar Ben Spackman, but rather that they are the words and thoughts of man that the Spirit approves in one form or another. They are the words which God has said bring us closer to him, and they are words that are life-giving. 
## What are the Scriptures?
Now that we have defined "Scripture," we can examine the nature of "The Scriptures." As stated above, scripture is not necessarily spoken by the mouth of God, but by his servants. These words combined with a holy seal of approval, form the basis of the scriptures as we have them. However, the scriptures are much more than just words that bring us closer to Christ. 

The scriptures are records according to the understandings of men. No place is this made clearer than in 1 Nephi 1:3, in which Nephi makes it clearer that his record is written "According to my knowledge." Other passages, which acknowledge the humanity of scripture are found in Nephi's apocalyptic writings. In 2 Nephi 27, Nephi describes a book comprised of "the words of them which have slumbered" (V. 6). This same book is described as being "[The Lord God's] word; and wo be unto him that rejecteth the word of God!" (V. 14). This also happens again in 2 Nephi 29, with a book presumed to be the Bible described in similar terms. 

However, no place is more clear about the valuable nature of scripture than the Title Page of the Book of Mormon itself, stating "And now, if there are faults they are the mistakes of men; Wherefore, condemn not the things of God, that ye may be found spotless at the judgement-seat of Christ." can't get much clearer than that.
# What have the Prophets said?
We've seen what crystallized revelation has said, but what about more modern, less filtered statements? I return first to the quote from earlier from Joseph Smith. 

‘I visited with a brother and sister from Michigan, who thought that “a prophet is always a prophet”; but I told them that a prophet was a prophet only when he was acting as such.'

His statement that prophets are human and speak often as humans without divine guidance is critical; we must understand first and foremost that Prophets are humans through whom God speaks. 

Elder D Todd Christofferson also spoke on this very subject in a talk in April 2012 entitled "The Doctrine of Christ." He argues that revelation is crucial and that ultimately all decisions made by the prophets and the church's councils are confirmed by revelation.  While this shows that the church's policies are not arbitrary, it also shows that oftentimes leadership seeks the best answer and then asks for confirmation, as opposed to being told how to proceed on every minute policy.
# What have Scholars said?
LDS Scholar and apologist Ben Spackman has written extensively on the nature of revelation in the church. He has a background in ancient Hebrew and early LDS history, and has written extensively about this subject. He has argued for 2 important concepts: Accommodation and Adaptation, that the Divine accommodates to the capacity of Humanity, and Adaptation, that the Human adapts the instruction of the Divine using human language 

While he has argued these 2 points fairly extensively in talks for FAIR, I will summarize these key arguments here: Accommodation is the adaptation of divine instruction down to the capacity of the human. Christ is recorded as saying that the law of Moses was diminished because of "the hardness of their hearts" (when teaching about the nature of divorce) and that those to whom the Law of Moses was given could not endure the higher law. 

The other side of the coin is adaptation, that humans take human stories and use them to teach divine principles. Spackman argues that a key example of this are the parallel but not identical creation stories in the Books of Abraham and Moses as well as in the Temple Endowment and Genesis. He argues that the non-iterative nature of the accounts' differences presents a challenge for an iterative or "pure revelation" model. Taken from a talk given at the fair conference:

"These differences between the texts themselves and examples from modern scripture undermine a simplistic equation of scripture with divinely revealed facts. They strongly suggest that revelation, ancient or modern, cannot be simplistically equated with “factual correctness.”"

# A Latter-Day Saint Framework of Revelation
A framework through which we can understand the declarations and trajectory of the Church can and must be reached. It is crucial that we understand this, so that we may understand the means, by which God communicates with us. A personal relationship with the Divine is the most important relationship that one may have. 

The Will of the Divine is made manifest to man, and that Will is subject to several factors. The request of man and capability of man are the 2 primary ones. This will is made known through revelation, and then adapted to a human understanding by the receiver. This expresses itself at the very least in putting "spiritual promptings" into human language, which subjects it to a level of interpretation and scrutiny. After this adaptation is made known, it is then interpreted by the Disciple and personally applied. 

Thus, the Instruction is Adapted to the capacity of all by the Divine, then Man translates to human language, then applies further direction and understanding, and then it is assumed. This instruction is mixed with useful rhetoric, and thus the essential Spiritual truths of Man's separation from God and his eventual return to him can be understood several different ways and times throughout the LDS experience.
