First Nephi 11-19 has the answers to a lot of questions. It's a wonderfully complex series of visions and travels that answer some of the most critical theological questions of our day. It also can be a great source of spiritual knowledge and reinforces the wonderful and unique beliefs that we hold as members of Christ's restored Church. 

For example, 11:6 is a critical verse that answers the question put forth for those that are skeptical about Moroni's challenge. For those that believe in Christ and his sacrifice, more will be revealed to those who seek revelation and truth. This was a question I often encountered on my mission, as creedal Christians tend to believe in the end of Public Revelation, but the Nephi shows that revelation are given to all who seek it and that this revelation can help us deepen our conversion and help our salvation.

Another critical Christian problem is presented in Philippians 2:5-9, in which Paul uses a controversial word, εκενωσεν, meaning to pour out or empty completely. It has a ritualistic context and creates an issue: for those who believe in the hypostatic union and dual-natured Christology, how does the singular person of Christ "pour out" himself while retaining his divinity and humanity? The angel answers in 11:16: Christ doesn't have 2 natures, but rather one: a divine nature, awaiting full realization through the passage of mortality. It's a unique and beautiful insight that answers one of the most ink-spilling questions of christianity.

Another crucial issue for Christians and non-Christians alike is the divinity of Christ. This is an issue that is raised by secularists, Unitarians, and Trinitarians. The statements of Christ's divinity in the bible have been argued back-and-forth for as long as there have been varying interpretations of holy writ. However, the Book of Mormon clearly explains the divine nature of Christ in 11:28, in which the angel proclaims "behold the condescension of God!" Immediately followed in verse 29 by the referent in the previous verse, being referred to as the "Lamb of God," a title reserved for Christ in both of the Bible and Book of Mormon.

My personal favorite question, one that I personally owe for my continued belief in God, is the matter of public revelation. A belief among every Christian group and most non-Christian groups is the cessation of public revelation, that is, that God spake, but does not speak today. It's a belief that I find rather hard to square with the proposition that God is a loving heavenly father, and indeed a certain philosophy has pervaded that God, in fact, is so powerful that he cannot be loving. However, 13:34 shows the love of God through his continual revelation. He has revealed the gospel, and yet we continue to misunderstand, misrepresent, and misinterpret, so he continues to speak and show us the way. He shows, through his mercy, that his desire is not to enact some divine puppet show, but rather to bring his children back to his presence.
