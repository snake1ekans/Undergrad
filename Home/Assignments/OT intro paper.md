I'm Gavin, I've taken New Testament with Dr Seely before. I'm from Ogden UT, served a mission in Miami Fl, and now I'm here studying as a premed student. I'm currently dating someone, but not close to engaged. I don't have a ton of spare time, but I do enjoy reading and playing video games.

I'm a huge fan of philosophy, so a lot of my favorite books and movies  are philosophical in nature. The one that's stuck with me most recently is a video game that's basically a book called disco elysium. It's a very existentialist work, and very hopeful in the end. Aside from that, I love tinkering so I'm always working on some old technology, old machines, or other weirdness. 

I'm taking Old Testament because I feel it's a bit of a blind spot for most Latter-day saints. That's really been exposed to me this year as I listened to people debate whether Job is a historic account or not, so I really hope to have the chance to have a more nuanced and complete understanding of the OT. After all, the past is a foreign country, they do things differently there.

My dream job would be to be a biomedical researcher with an MD-PhD and the freedom to investigate whatever questions interest me, which is something I'm working at to some extent, although it's difficult to get to that point right now. I am a very curious person, and having the opportunity to learn for a living would be amazing.

I think the biggest piece of wisdom I would share with my children is that existence has no inherent and self-dependent meaning. It's an existentialist idea, but the notion that existence is not in and of itself purpose is a powerful notion that has gotten me out of bed on more than one occasion. It's also the thing that drives me to be faithful in the "pistis" sense, the idea that in order to become godly I must participate in godly acts to build Zion is one that I hold quite sacred.

A place that I have a deep love for is the city of Miami. It's where I spent 2 years of my life, but more than that, it's a place where I matured greatly and learned some of my most important life lessons. It's trite to say that a mission changed you or that it was the best 2 years, and I think a lot of people overplay their hand when they say that, but I will say that the time that I spent with the people in Miami changed a lot of my perspectives about the church, about the world, and about myself. And on top of everything, the food is amazing.
