I think the thing that struck me the most was the ways in which the kids growing up in poverty were aware of their situations and also naive at the same time. Their thoughts are very aware of the broad implications of their situation, but they can't explain the minutia of paying bills and bad truck brakes. I think it's also very telling because so often Americans view poverty as a personal moral failing, but it's very clearly a result of the system. The people in the documentary are fundamentally good people, but their circumstances mean that they aren't able to improve their situations. As teens, these people had to deal with the results of growing up poor, which meant taking a lot of risks and dealing with that understanding and growing into it very differently. I think, however, the biggest theme of the whole documentary is the humanity of everybody in the doc. Seeing the ways in which these universal emotions like grief are shaped by the situation in which people find themselves is both touching and a good reminder of the universality of so much of human experience. Roger's passing and jaycee's stillbirth touched me because of the ways in which they were integral to their families. So much of the documentary is overshadowed by the families' financial situations, but underneath or perhaps despite it all is that persistence of family and humanity. Poverty is a tricky thing, and despite some of these folks' best efforts, they weren't able to improve their situations as much as they would like because they were trapped in the **cycle of poverty**. I think this is a great documentary to discuss the ways in which poverty shapes families and lives because it's very easy to imagine how these people would have different lives if they didn't grow up impoverished. I appreciated that despite the specter of poverty across the whole documentary, humanity was the primary focus and a reminder that victims of poverty are ultimately people, not just statistics or "others."
